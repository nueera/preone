PreOne · Access & Roles

Who Holds Which Key?

A first-principles tour of everyone who can walk into the PreOne preschool app — the door their key opens, and where a few doors sit quietly propped open.

Start from zero

A school is a building. A role is a key.

Picture PreOne as a big, sunny preschool. Inside are rooms full of precious things — children's records, medical notes, fee ledgers, private chats between parents and teachers. You can't let just anyone wander in, so the building asks every visitor one question first:

“Who are you, and what may you touch?”

PreOne answers with a small paper name-tag called a token. When you log in, the office hands you a tag with your name, your role and your branch, then presses a wax seal so it can't be forged. Technically that's base64url(payload).HMAC-SHA256, it expires after 24 hours, and you carry a copy in a cookie (preone_token) and in the browser's pocket.

The wax seal is the whole game. Every gate below trusts the tag only because the seal matches — and the seal is made with a secret key. As we'll see, PreOne keeps a spare key under the doormat for when nobody set a real one.

Three gates guard every room

Reaching a piece of data means passing up to three checkpoints. This layering is the healthiest part of the design — no single lock has to be perfect.

01

The Front Gate

The middleware checks your tag on every request. No tag on a page → sent to /login; no tag on an API → 401. It also runs a quick, coarse role check.

02

The Classroom Door

Each route re-opens the tag itself (requireAdmin, requireRole…). It never trusts hallway headers — it re-checks the wax seal from scratch.

03

The Cubby Lock

Even inside, you reach only your things: a parent sees only their own children, a teacher only their class, an admin only their branch.

Meet the cast

Everyone who can walk in the door

The Admin — “Principal”

Role ADMIN · portal /admin · created at sign-up
weight 100

The Admin is born the moment a school signs up: POST /api/auth/register creates a School and its first ADMIN in one breath. From then on the Principal holds the master ring — nearly every room opens to them.

What they can do

  • Enroll and edit students, teachers, classes and programs.
  • Run the money — fees, invoices, payments, reminders, refunds.
  • Mark and review attendance; read/write growth observations & milestones.
  • Broadcast announcements, run chat, manage users & school settings.
  • See the whole dashboard, reports, admissions pipeline and calendar.

Where the keys reach

/api/students/api/teachers/api/fees/api/attendance/api/growth/api/communication/api/settings/api/reports/api/dashboard/api/events

How far they see

Branch isolation decides how much of the school an Admin sees. An Admin whose tag carries a branchId is scoped to that campus. But an Admin with no branch is treated as “all branches” and sees everything — the closest thing PreOne has to a super-user, created implicitly rather than on purpose.

The Task Master — “Front Desk”

Role TASK_MASTER · borrows /admin · fenced in
weight 80

The Task Master has no building of their own — they're handed a visitor pass to the Principal's portal, with tape on the floor marking where they may walk. They're the admissions & reception clerk: chase leads, message families, watch the dashboard.

Where they may walk

The front gate fences them to four hallways (TASK_MASTER_ALLOWED_PREFIXES). Wander off, and they're bounced back to Admissions.

/admin/dashboard/admin/admissions/admin/communication/chat/admin/communication/announcements

What their key opens

/api/admissions/api/crm/api/dashboard/api/auth/me

Students, fees, teachers, settings? Locked — those routes demand a full ADMIN key and answer with 403.

The Teacher — “Class Teacher”

Role TEACHER · portal /teacher · one classroom
weight 50

The Teacher lives in their own bright portal. When they knock, PreOne doesn't just check the tag — it looks up their staff record and finds the one class assigned to them (requireTeacher → assignedClass). Everything they do stays inside those lines.

What they can do

  • Take daily attendance for their class.
  • Write observations and daily updates (meals, nap, mood).
  • Record growth notes — the one “grown-up” API they share with Admin.
  • Chat with their class's families and apply for leave.

What their key opens

/api/teacher/*/api/growth/api/auth/me

Reach for the admin wing and the front gate returns 403 before they touch the handle.

The Parent — “Guardian”

Role PARENT · portal /parent · one family
weight 30

The Parent has the smallest, most tender key — and the tightest cubby lock. On every request PreOne finds their guardian record, gathers the children linked to them, then refuses any child not on that list (verifyChildAccess → 403). A parent literally cannot peek at another family's little one.

What they can do

  • See their own child's daily updates, growth passport, attendance and memories.
  • View fees and pay; read announcements.
  • Chat with their child's teacher; upload KYC documents.
  • Tune their own notification preferences (app / SMS / email).

What their key opens

/api/parent/*/api/auth/me

The fragile bit

A Parent has no direct link to their login account. PreOne re-finds them each time by matching the login email against Parent.email or Parent.phone — an identity handshake made of string-matching rather than a firm ID.

Mind the gap

Doors that sit quietly propped open

None of these are “the sky is falling.” They're the ordinary soft spots of a young codebase — a spare key here, a guest list left on the counter there. Worst first.

A spare key under the doormat

CRITICAL
src/lib/auth.ts · src/middleware.ts

The wax seal is made with JWT_SECRET || TOKEN_SECRET || 'preone-demo-secret-key-2024'. If neither env secret is set in production, the app silently signs every token with a public, hard-coded string — anyone who knows it can forge a valid ADMIN tag and pass all three gates.

Fix: refuse to boot in production if no secret is configured — never fall back to a shipped default.
Show the evidence
const JWT_SECRET = process.env.JWT_SECRET
  || process.env.TOKEN_SECRET
  || 'preone-demo-secret-key-2024';  // ← forgeable if envs unset

A guest list anyone logged-in can read

HIGH
/api/settings/users (GET) — not covered by the front gate's role map

The coarse role map (API_ROUTE_RULES) lists only ~13 route families. Sensitive ones — /api/settings, /api/reports, /api/events, /api/branches — aren't in it, so the gate merely checks “is this tag valid?” and lets any role through. The user-directory listing then authenticates with getAuthUser (any logged-in user) instead of requireAdmin — so a Parent or Teacher token can list every user's name, email and role.

Fix: add /api/settings, /api/reports, /api/system to the route map and upgrade those handlers from getAuthUser to requireAdmin.

The ghost hall-monitor

HIGH
middleware.ts defines it · auth.ts can never mint it

The front gate treats SuperAdmin as the top rank — the only role allowed into /admin/system and /api/system. But the token layer knows only four roles, and verifyToken rejects any tag whose role isn't one of them. So no real person can ever be a SuperAdmin — the whole system-admin wing is a locked room nobody can enter. Two layers disagree about how many keys exist.

Fix: decide deliberately — promote SUPER_ADMIN to a real role in the Prisma enum + token validator, or delete the phantom references so the “top tier” isn't a fiction.
Show the mismatch
// middleware.ts — five names, SuperAdmin is highest
type RoleName = 'Admin'|'Teacher'|'Parent'|'TaskMaster'|'SuperAdmin';
{ pattern: /^\/api\/system/, allowedRoles: ['SuperAdmin'] }

// auth.ts — only four are real; anything else fails
export const ALL_ROLES = [ADMIN, TEACHER, PARENT, TASK_MASTER];
if (!ALL_ROLES.includes(role)) return null; // SuperAdmin never verifies

“No branch” quietly means “all branches”

HIGH
branch isolation · getBranchFromRequest

Branch scoping keys off whether an Admin's tag carries a branchId. Leave it blank and the Admin is promoted to see every branch in the school. There's no separate “branch admin” vs “head-office admin” role — cross-campus visibility is decided by an absent field. Worse, a few models (Lead, FeeStructure, Event, Holiday) carry no school/branch column at all, so they're global across every tenant on the deployment.

Fix: make org-wide access an explicit role, not a missing value; add a tenant column to the global models.

A hall-pass that can't be torn up

MEDIUM
verifyToken checks signature + 24h expiry only

There's no server-side session or revocation list. Logging out only empties the browser's pockets — the signed tag stays valid its full 24 hours. A leaked or stolen token can't be cancelled. (A RefreshToken table exists, but the verifier never consults it.)

Fix: shorten access-token life and check a server-side refresh / denylist so “log out everywhere” actually works.

Guardian identity held together with string-matching

MEDIUM
api-auth.ts · requireParent

A Parent record has no foreign key to its login. Each request re-links them by matching the login email to Parent.email or Parent.phone. Shared numbers, recycled emails, or a typo could resolve one login to the wrong guardian — and the cubby lock is only as trustworthy as that match.

Fix: add a real Parent.userId foreign key and match on it.

The front door signs up instant principals

MEDIUM
/api/auth/register (public)

Anyone can create a School and an active ADMIN in one unauthenticated call — the new admin is isActive: true immediately, with no email verification first. The good news: the role is hard-coded to ADMIN, so you can't self-escalate higher through the request body. The risk is abuse and unverified accounts, not privilege injection.

Fix: require email/OTP verification before activation, and rate-limit + captcha the endpoint.
Room for more friends

New name-tags worth printing

PreOne runs a whole preschool on four keys. A real school has more shapes of grown-up. First principles: give a role to every distinct “need-to-touch,” so the master key stops being the answer to everything.

Super Admin / Owner

Make the ghost real: cross-branch, billing, and the /admin/system wing that's currently unreachable.

Branch vs School Admin

Split “runs one campus” from “runs the org,” so wide access is a choice — not a blank field.

Accountant / Fee Desk

Fees, invoices, payments, refunds — and nothing else. Today only the all-powerful Admin touches money.

Nurse / Caregiver

Medical records and daily care (meals, nap, meds) — sensitive health data deserves its own narrow key.

Academic Head

Read-mostly oversight of growth, attendance and reports across classes, without edit rights.

Co-Guardian (view-only)

A second parent or grandparent with read access. The isPrimary flag exists but isn't used for permissions yet.

Auditor

Read-only access plus the audit log — for compliance, without the ability to change anything.

Transport Coordinator

Routes, vehicles, pickup/drop. The data models exist; no role or portal uses them yet.
